# 05.08 Requirements & Lifecycle: Teaching 1 (about 8 min)

Source: Developer Module 5, screen 8 of 25. Title: "From business requirements to functional and infrastructure requirements".

Later deployment-platform decisions assume requirements already exist (residency rule, latency target, identity model). This screen is where they come from: turning a business problem into functional and infrastructure requirements a deployment decision can be defended against.

## Functional requirements
Say what the system must do, with enough detail to check. A business problem ("help support agents answer faster") isn't yet a requirement. Derived functional requirements look like: "classify each ticket into one of four queues; draft a reply citing the relevant policy; never auto-send without human approval". Write each as a checkable statement of behaviour: a vague goal can't be designed against or verified, a specific one becomes a line in an eval and a criterion at review.

## Infrastructure requirements
Non-functional constraints the deployment must satisfy. Mostly not stated in the business problem; derive them by asking the questions it implies:
- **Latency:** how fast must a response be, measured where the user is?
- **Scale:** how many requests, and what peak?
- **Residency:** where must data be processed, under which regulation?
- **Identity:** who acts, under what credentials, and what must be auditable?
These four most often decide the deployment platform; capture them at the start, before a platform is chosen for other reasons.

## Document them so the decision is defensible
The deployment decision will be reviewed by people who didn't gather the requirements. A short requirements record (functional behaviours, infrastructure constraints, and the regulation each constraint comes from) lets you defend a platform choice as following from requirements rather than familiarity. It is the input to the next deployment decision (see 05.12).

- **Handles well:** turns a business problem into checkable functional and infrastructure requirements before any platform is chosen.
- **Adds cost:** eliciting infrastructure constraints takes a scoping conversation teams are tempted to skip.
- **Use something else:** a throwaway prototype with no review and no regulated data needs only lightweight notes.
