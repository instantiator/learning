# 05.23 Recap: Key Takeaways (about 3 min)

Source: Developer Module 5, screen 23 of 25. Title: "Key takeaways".

1. **Package while the build is fresh.** An accelerator keeps the reusable logic, exposes customer-specific parts as documented parameters, and bundles the eval and the audit log. Correct packaging yields an asset teams configure. Knowing what's customer-specific is most expensive to reconstruct after the people who held it have moved on.
2. **A maintainer accepts what they can verify.** Match the asset to the channel built for its shape, then clear the bar: focused code, a runnable example, a test, a statement of assumptions, with licensing rights confirmed before technical review. A contribution a reviewer can't verify sits at the back of the queue.
3. **Pin what ships.** Choose the platform from the customer's cloud and compliance posture; pin the specific model version, not the moving alias, and keep the prior version available. Analogy: an alias is "the current edition of a book" (convenient, but the text can change); pinning cites a fixed edition, so an upstream change is adopted deliberately, not overnight with no rollback path.
4. **Measure the dimension that decides the placement.** A platform choice is defensible only when latency (from the customer's region), compliance (against their existing certification) and cost (total per call, not token price alone) are measured. For regulated customers compliance is usually pass-or-fail; raise it in scoping so it can't reject the build at contract review.
5. **Mark every seam as a boundary.** A multi-component app is only as contained as its most privileged seam. Scope each component to the minimum access; treat every data crossing as a trust boundary; fetched content is data, not instructions. Trust at a boundary must be established explicitly; it doesn't carry over from the sender. If a seam can't be secured, it goes to a human owner rather than shipping.

## What comes next
You can now package a build into a reusable asset, contribute it back, place and version it on the right platform, defend that placement, and connect components so boundaries hold. That completes the build-to-deploy arc for this persona: from production code in the earlier modules to assets a regulated customer can audit and a team can reuse.
