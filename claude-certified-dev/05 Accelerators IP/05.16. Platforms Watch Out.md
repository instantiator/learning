# 05.16 Comparing Platforms: Watch Out (about 2 min)

Source: Developer Module 5, screen 16 of 25. Title: "The platform picked on familiarity that failed residency". Told as an anecdote a developer shares after a review goes sideways.

## The scenario
A team picked the platform they already knew because migration looked easy and the deadline was close. It built fine; but easy-to-build and allowed-to-ship are different criteria.

## What happened
A developer building for a regulated customer chose the platform the team had shipped on before. Integration came together fast and passed functional tests. At the customer's security review, the reviewer asked where data was processed, and the platform didn't satisfy the residency requirement. A platform the team knew less well would have met it through regional deployment options the customer had already cleared. The placement was rejected and the integration rebuilt.

## Why it broke
Familiarity optimised for the wrong test. Easy migration answered "can we build quickly?", never "will this pass the customer's residency review?", which determines whether it can ship. Because compliance wasn't settled in scoping, it arrived at the go/no-go review: the costliest place to find out, since the build was done.

## What to watch for
A platform easy for your team isn't necessarily one the customer may run. For a regulated customer, residency and compliance are often pass-or-fail, not trade-offs. Identify them in scoping and let them drive placement before familiarity does. Checking early costs a scoping conversation; checking late costs a rebuild.
