# 04.04 Evals & Judges: Checkpoint (about 9 min)

Source: Developer Module 4, screen 4 of 23. Title: "Complete a partial eval for a summarization feature". A drag-and-drop matching exercise, not submitted; the answers below are the ones 04.02 and 04.03 support.

## The task
A partial eval has two gaps. In the dataset, say what output each input case should produce; in the judge prompt, match each score band to its meaning. Drag answer cards onto the rows.

## The partial eval (described)
- **Dataset** with three cases: (1) a long support thread about a delayed refund, expected: a two-sentence summary naming the issue (delayed refund) and the current status (escalated); (2) a meeting transcript where three action items are assigned, expected behaviour blank; (3) a bug report with repro steps and one unrelated aside, expected behaviour blank.
- **Judge prompt:** grade a summary against its expected behaviour, returning `strengths`, `weaknesses`, `reasoning` and `score`, with score bands 1 to 3, 4 to 7 and 8 to 10 to define.
- Answer cards: "a summary that lists all three action items with their owners"; "a summary of the bug and its repro steps that omits the unrelated aside"; "misses required content"; "partial: some required content present, some missing"; "complete and faithful to the expected behaviour".

## Matches
| Row | Answer |
|---|---|
| Expected output, meeting-transcript case | A summary that lists all three action items with their owners |
| Expected output, bug-report case | A summary of the bug and its repro steps that omits the unrelated aside |
| Judge score band 1 to 3 | Misses required content |
| Judge score band 4 to 7 | Partial: some required content present, some missing |
| Judge score band 8 to 10 | Complete and faithful to the expected behaviour |

## Why
Expected behaviours must be gradable and specific, like the first case (04.02's "two-sentence summary listing every action item and its owner"). Defining what each score band means is how you tighten a judge's rubric so its scores line up with human labels (calibration, 04.02); without it models drift to a safe middle number.
