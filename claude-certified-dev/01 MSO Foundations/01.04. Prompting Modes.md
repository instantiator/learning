# 01.04 Prompting Modes (about 8 min)

Source: Developer Module 1, screen 4 of 9. Three tabs: The Three Modes, Cost & Quality Trade-off, Mode & Model Choice.

## The three modes
How many worked examples you put in the prompt is a separate matter from how you word the instruction.
- **Zero-shot:** instruction only, no examples.
- **One-shot:** one example of input paired with the desired output.
- **Multi-shot (few-shot):** several such examples.
- The examples are not training data. They sit in the prompt and show the exact shape of the answer you want, which a description alone often fails to pin down.

## Cost vs quality
- Every example costs tokens on every call and uses up context budget.
- Use zero-shot when the task is simple and the output shape is obvious.
- Move to one-shot or multi-shot when the output needs a specific structure, casing or edge-case handling that a description keeps missing. One or two correct examples often fix this faster than another paragraph of instructions.
- Discipline (reinforced in Module 2): add the smallest amount of prompt that gives a reliable result.

## Interaction with model choice
- A more capable model may succeed zero-shot where a smaller one needs a few examples. Adding examples can let a cheaper model do the job.
- Decide the two together: try the simplest model with the fewest examples that meets your eval, and add capability or examples only where the eval says you must.
